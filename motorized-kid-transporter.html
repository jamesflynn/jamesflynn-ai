Personal Project

Motorized Kid Transporter

An electric drivetrain for a Veer City Cruiser XL — built at Artisans Asylum.

The idea

Hauling several kids and their gear across a city is a solved problem if you own a cargo bike and a place to park it. On a sidewalk, at walking pace, with a wagon, it is not: pull-wagons are heavy before you add children, and every hill is a negotiation.

So I'm adding an electric motor to the Veer City Cruiser XL — a stroller-wagon that's already built like a small vehicle — to make the first sidewalk-worthy electric kid transporter of its kind.

The build

  • The wagon's front axle and pull handle come off; a mobility scooter's front end goes on in their place — steering column, single driven wheel, and tiller.
  • Scooter controls carry over intact: key switch, speed dial, and thumb throttle on the handlebars, so the whole thing is operated from behind at walking pace.
  • Battery and controller live in a plywood box under the front of the frame, low and centered to keep it from getting tippy.
  • The wagon's seats, harnesses, and mesh sides stay stock — the kids' half of the vehicle was already the well-engineered half.
  • Fabrication at Artisans Asylum in Allston, MA.

Status

Rolling prototype — it steers and drives, and the gaffer tape on the tiller is load-bearing. Still to do: a proper enclosure for the battery box, and brakes I actually trust with cargo this precious.
